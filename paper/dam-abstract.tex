Every lower bound on the Shannon capacity $\Theta(C_7)$ since July 2026 rests on gadgets in
$C_7^{\boxtimes 5}$: a code, a family of \emph{private pairs} (a vertex $q$ outside the code with a
single neighbour $r$ in it), and an auxiliary independent set that must avoid a forbidden region and is
rewarded for avoiding a penalised one. The bound is most sensitive to the number of private pairs. We
study that number as a characteristic of a code and show why it is not, by itself, a lever. The two
regions a pair creates are the intersection and the union of two closed neighbourhoods, so their sizes
add up to $2\cdot 3^d$: the shape of a pair moves cost between them but cannot lower it. For the
Polak--Schrijver code, on which all the recent bounds are built, we prove that exactly eight private
pairs exist and a ninth does not. For the codes with nine and ten pairs that we found, the forbidden
regions are fixed by the geometry of the pairs: up to automorphism there are sixteen at nine pairs, the
same for every such code, and against all of them the best auxiliary set we could build has $366$ words,
one short --- exactly $3$-opt optimal, and not proved maximum. We tabulate the five shapes of a pair
with their costs and the candidate counts of every code we hold.
